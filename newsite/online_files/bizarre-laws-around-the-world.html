📖Reading

When Laws Go Wrong

"The law is an ass." - Charles Dickens

1. Every country has its share of bizarre laws that seem to make no sense in modern times. These outdated regulations often remain on the books for decades, even centuries, long after their original purpose has been forgotten. While most are never enforced, they can still cause confusion and amusement for both locals and visitors.

2. In Singapore, it's illegal to sell chewing gum without a prescription. This law was introduced in 1992 to keep the streets clean, but it has become one of the country's most famous regulations. The penalty for breaking this law can be quite severe, including fines and even jail time for repeat offenders.

3. Switzerland has a law that makes it illegal to flush the toilet after 10 PM in apartment buildings. This regulation was created to reduce noise pollution, but it seems rather bizarre to most people. The law is still technically in effect, though it's rarely enforced in practice.

4. In Denmark, it's against the law to burn foreign flags, but burning the Danish flag is perfectly legal. This contradictory law reflects the country's complex relationship with national symbols and freedom of expression. Many Danes find this regulation confusing and illogical.

5. Some laws are so old that they've become completely irrelevant. In the UK, it's still technically illegal to handle a salmon in suspicious circumstances, a law that dates back to the 19th century. While these laws might seem funny, they highlight how difficult it can be to repeal outdated legislation and keep legal systems up to date.

✍️Grammar Review — Obligation Modals

1. In Singapore, you (must/have to) get a prescription to buy chewing gum.

2. Swiss residents (mustn't/shouldn't) flush toilets after 10 PM.

3. Danish citizens (can/may) burn their own flag legally.

4. You (don't have to/mustn't) follow laws that are never enforced.

5. Governments (should/ought to) review outdated laws regularly.
